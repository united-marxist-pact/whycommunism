spoken · the reader's introductionThis is the first video in the series: The Communist Mode of Production According to Marx and Engels. This is the Preface and Format of it, which I have subtitled: Establishment of the True Human Community and Commonwealth.

This project was created with the intent of getting down to brass tacks about what Marx and Engels really had to say about the character of the Communist Mode of Production across the board, in all sections of it. You may have heard that, in the past, Marx and Engels said very little about Communism and instead confined themselves to critiquing Capitalism. While this is true to some degree as the absolute vast majority of their writings were about Capitalism, it must be understood that Communism is the Real Movement that abolishes the present state of things, the negation of the negation which creates the basis for the next stage of historical development in the process of human emancipation and rehabilitation. It is the riddle of history solved- the resolution of history as it is the necessary form and dynamic principle of the immediate future, who’s conditions for existence result from the premises now in existence. It is not a state of affairs to be established as a modeled future, created once and for all, but the real movement towards the real human community through the free association of equal producers wherein the basis for the development of the individual is the condition for the development of all, wherein the social division of labor is ended, alienation ceases, the productive forces and individual producers reach high development, and thus Bourgeois Right is escaped in its entirety as society inscribes on its banners “From each according to his ability, to each according to his needs!”

FROM EACH ACCORDING TO HIS ABILITY · TO EACH ACCORDING TO HIS NEEDS

Because of this, while they did not make a large amount of concrete claims directly about the character of the Communist Mode of Production, they still did through Dialectical or “reciprocal action” Materialist, economic equivalence-theoretical, biological-metamorphic, and feedback-disruptive logical methods. By analyzing material characteristics and resulting cultural and social forms, along with philosophic ones stemming from the Humanist tradition, Marx’s intent becomes more clear. In addition, in a variety of texts they indeed made direct explanations about the character of the next Mode of Production, if in a generalist form, leaving much of the particulars to those who would come after (which we may look at later). I have a list of reading resources for anybody who wants to take a look. One thing this is missing, due to being read in English rather than original German, is that it leaves out a large amount of specific terminology utilized by Marx which is in reference to past philosophers (and thus drawing on the concepts they utilized) and repetitious phrases he utilized to draw connections to concepts but from different perspectives, which this series will engage with to some degree later on- I have done the best that I can to express these in my own summaries where I had knowledge of them at the time of writing. And while doing these readings, I may give additional input as well. The post-Marx and Engels texts provided below will tend to address these sorts of things, giving specification and carrying out proper academic work.

The Format of this is principally by year. I have read through the below texts and extracted quotations that I found pertinent to what Marx and Engels said in terms of the Communist Mode of Production. I tried to avoid repetition where possible, and summarized the quotes, followed by utilizing my notes to construct a summary. Thus, the summaries tend to be in very down-to-earth words trying to express what is being said in the most direct way possible, though sometimes I add in my own thoughts. At the end of this, I will be producing a PowerPoint presentation to try and express the totality of what Marx and Engels had to say about it. This list is intended to cover some of the areas that Marx was most vague, principally in terms of the actual carrying out of Economic Planning. While Marx did give generalities about what planning should involve, such as the inputs needed and the ways in which production and distribution will be simplified, the particulars are left in the hands of others as Engels expressed in 1890 - Engels to Conrad Schmidt In Berlin, August 5. The last piece in our reading list here. In addition, it intends to clean up the misunderstandings that developed with various historic Socialist experiments in terms of the content of the Communist programme.

This also seeks to explicate more in terms of some of the more esoteric aspects of Marx, such as the particularities of his (Post-)Humanist philosophical views and the humanizing project of Communism, the Gemeinschaft (or Community as Association, the means of man’s humanization of himself), the theory of Alienation applied specifically to Nature in terms of the Metabolic Rift, specifics on the Family when Classed society ends, etc. There is much in the Marx and Engels reading list which does not truly address these key aspects to understanding what Marx intends with the Communist Mode of Production, which is fundamentally a means to an end which is that truly human community. I may construct summaries of some of these texts later, but in the meantime, simply understand that I do a poor job in expressing the real content of the Communist project due to lack of proper academic methodology. The texts below cover for that, generally focusing on particular aspects of Marx.
