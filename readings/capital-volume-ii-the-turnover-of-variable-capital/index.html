spoken · the reader's introductionWelcome to the ninth video in the series, The Character of the Communist Mode of Production According to Marx and Engels. Today we’ll be going over the 1885 piece by Marx, Capital Volume Two, Chapter 16, The Turnover of Variable Capital. I have subtitled this “Planned Production and Distribution Overcomes Capitalist Limits.”

All systems of production must continuously reproduce the conditions necessary for their own existence—this includes the mode of production, which is made up of its specific forces and relations of production. Communism is no exception; it must sustain and expand itself by developing its unique mode of production, thereby reinforcing the forces and relations of production distinctive to it, in contrast to other systems. These elements form the basis of its laws of motion. This process of reproducing its own conditions serves as the material foundation of Communism, and as the mode of production advances, this cycle of renewal and expansion deepens and intensifies.

In Capitalism, Exploitation is not embedded within the amount paid to the worker in money, but rather in the wage relation and mode of production itself; all surplus-product becomes surplus-value, and thereby with or without transference into Money or actual sale of the products produced, it is still exploitation, because surplus-value extraction occurs during production itself. The amount of surplus-value that can be produced depends on the magnitude of variable capital employed and degree of exploitation of labor, but the amount of variable capital that can be employed relies on advanced money-capital. Therefore, in Capitalism, money-capital determines the rate of surplus-value, within the larger macro-constraint on production that is possible under Capitalism. However, this is not necessary. In Communism, surplus-value is not produced; rather, instead surplus-product might be. The amount of labor employed by it is not constrained by anything but labor itself in the most direct sense, thus allowing the maximum cap on production possible. There is no money-capital which binds the direction of labor, and so Communist society calculates the raw inputs of any project, no matter how large or long, and determines socially if it should proceed. Communism is naturally capable of long-term planning and organizing.

In addition, in Capitalist society, projects that take significant amounts of time without producing surplus-value, like railways or power plants, are very difficult to justify producing by individual capitalists. In Communism, though, because there is no money-capital or the binding of the direction of labor to such, all Communist society needs to do is:

1. Calculate how much Labor, Means of Production, and Means of Subsistence can be invested in the project without detriment to the rest of society 2. Extract Labor, Means of Production, and Means of Subsistence from total annual production to support the prolonged project

As such, Communism thrives on, and is extremely capable of this sort of long-term planning and development. While Capitalist society must jump through all sorts of hoops to engage in developmental projects, Communism does it as a matter of course, in its own natural reproductive system. In Capitalism, social reason asserts itself after the fact, and it is constantly vulnerable to changes in the market which can stop development, since any such long term project requires constant and long-term advancements.

spoken · failed malls and abandoned projectsAnd I’m sure that we all are aware of failed malls and projects that end up being done nothing on for months or even years, possibly even decades, and then getting abandoned halfway through, even completely. Continuing.

Projects that have long turnovers, and therefore do not immediately produce surplus-value, still require the workers to be paid so they can maintain themselves. Thus, as workers pay money into the market for Means of Subsistence while not actually adding to the stock, demand increases without supply also increasing. As a result, productive materials and Means of Subsistence rise in price, and from that, speculators start emerging in the field of productive materials and Means of Subsistence, further artificially inflating the demand for these. Despite this, production is not based on demand, it is based on material capacity for production- as examples, plants don't grow faster just because speculators increase demand. Rather, it simply results in demand for imports, and in comparison to such, industry that can be expanded like mining results in a sudden increase in production which, in turn, results in overproduction when speculation goes over expectation. Prices rise and then immediately collapse in regular boom/bust cycles. Hence, a continually widening division between town and country, core and periphery, uneven and combined development.

This also occurs in the Labor Market: these new mines and whatnot attract many, and absorbs some of the reserve army of labor. This in turn causes a general rise in wages, but is temporary: the businesses then crash, labor is set loose, and wages reduce maximally. Further, Capitalism also seeks to maximize the price of commodities on the market, but minimize the price of labor-power. Thus, the workers can't buy their own products and can buy less and less of it. This makes capitalists less capable of selling their products, which leads to greater overproduction. Capitalism also is unable to actually reach full production potential because any time it seeks to it leads to this overproduction. The sale of commodities, and thus the realization of surplus-value, is limited by the fact that society cannot actually buy everything it produces. The period necessary to get any given product ready for the market is materially determined; it is based on existing material conditions of production specific for that product. Therefore, the market is a post-hoc recognition of Value and can only ever be such. Communism, on the other hand, has no such need of such post-hoc recognition, as it determines it through labor directly

In Communism, in comparison, there is no such speculation, no such supply and demand shifts. The Means of Subsistence are understood directly and socially consumed, while production is in line with material reality and is planned, without any such boom/bust cycles. Labor consumes all necessary means of subsistence, and since overproduction is impossible since Communist society can consume all that it produces, that means that there ceases any and all fetters on consumption aside from society's productive capacity itself. Hence, people will have access to more under Communism than they ever would under Capitalism.

QUOTATION EXTRACTS

Whatever the form of the process of production in a society, it must be a continuous process, must continue to go periodically through the same phases... When viewed therefore as a connected whole and as flowing on with incessant renewal, every social process of production is, at the same time, a process of reproduction... As a periodic increment of the capital advanced, or periodic fruit of capital in process, surplus-value acquires the form of a revenue flowing out of capital.

  1. All systems of production must reproduce themselves
  2. This reproduction must be continuous and repeat the same phases over and over again
  3. This continuous and repetitious process grows and increases with the development of the mode of production

The conversion, sooner or later, of the value-substitute into money, and thus into the form in which variable capital is advanced, is obviously an immaterial circumstance, so far as the production of surplus-value itself is concerned. This production depends on the magnitude of variable capital employed and the degree of exploitation of labour. But that circumstance modifies the magnitude of the money-capital which must be advanced in order to set a definite quantum of labour-power in motion during the year, and therefore it determines the annual rate of surplus-value.

  1. Exploitation is not embedded without the amount paid in money, but rather in the wage relation and the mode of production itself
  2. Surplus-Value production happening at all, with or without transference into Money or actual sale of the products produced, is still exploitation, because surplus-value extraction occurs during production itself
  3. The amount of surplus-value that can be produced depends on the magnitude of variable capital employed and degree of exploitation of labor, but the amount of variable capital that can be employed relies on advanced money-capital. This is because labor is the macro-constraint on production
  4. Therefore, money-capital determines the rate of surplus-value
  5. In Communism, surplus-value is not produced (though surplus product might be). The amount of labor employed is not constrained by the amount of money-capital society can apply to a project. Thus, it is capable of being far more productive.

Second — and this is interlinked with the first difference — the B and A labourers pay for the means of subsistence which they buy with the variable capital that has been transformed in their hands into a medium of circulation. For instance they not only withdraw wheat from the market, but they also replace it with an equivalent in money. But since the money wherewith the B labourer pays for his means of subsistence, which he withdraws from the market, is not the money-form of a value produced and thrown by him on the market during the year, as it is in the case of the A labourer, he supplies the seller of the means of subsistence with money, but not with commodities — be they means of production or means of subsistence — which this seller could buy with the proceeds of the sale, as he can in the case of A. The market is therefore stripped of labour-power, means of subsistence for this labour-power, fixed capital in the form of instruments of labour used in the case of B, and of materials of production, and to replace them an equivalent in money is thrown on the market; but during the year no product is thrown on the market with which to replace the material elements of productive capital withdrawn from it. If we conceive society as being not capitalistic but communistic, there will be no money-capital at all in the first place, not the disguises cloaking the transactions arising on account of it. The question then comes down to the need of society to calculate beforehand how much labour, means of production, and means of subsistence it can invest, without detriment, in such lines of business as for instance the building of railways, which do not furnish any means of production or subsistence, nor produce any useful effect for a long time, a year or more, while they extract labour, means of production and means of subsistence from the total annual production. In capitalist society however where social reason always asserts itself only post festum great disturbances may and must constantly occur. On the one hand pressure is brought to bear on the money-market, while on the other, an easy money-market calls such enterprises into being en masse, thus creating the very circumstances which later give rise to pressure on the money-market. Pressure is brought to bear on the money-market, since large advances of money-capital are constantly needed here for long periods of time. And this regardless of the fact that industrialists and merchants throw the money-capital necessary to carry on their business into speculative railway schemes; etc., and make it good by borrowing in the money-market.

  1. In Capitalism, projects that take significant amounts of time without producing surplus-value, like railway or powerplant production, are very difficult to justify for individual capitals.
  2. In Communism, there will be no money-capital, and no binding of the direction of labor to it. All Communist society needs to do is:

a. Calculate how much Labor, Means of Production, and Means of Subsistence to invest without detriment

    b. Labor, MoP and MoS are extracted from total annual production to support the prolonged project

      c. Communism thus thrives on and is extremely capable of this sort of long-term planning and development

      1. In Capitalism, social reason asserts itself after the fact, and it is constantly vulnerable to changes in the market which can stop development since any such project requires constant and long term advancements

      On the other hand pressure on society’s available productive capital. Since elements of productive capital are for ever being withdrawn from the market and only an equivalent in money is thrown on the market in their place, the effective demand rises without itself furnishing any element of supply. Hence a rise in the prices of productive materials as well as means of subsistence. To this must be added that stock-jobbing is a regular practice and capital is transferred on a large scale. A band of speculators, contractors, engineers, lawyers, etc., enrich themselves. They create a strong demand for articles of consumption on the market, wages rising at the same time. So far as foodstuffs are involved, agriculture too is stimulated. But as these foodstuffs cannot be suddenly increased in the course of the year, their import grows, just as that of exotic foods in general (coffee, sugar, wine, etc.) and of articles of luxury. Hence excessive imports and speculation in this line of the import business. Meanwhile, in those branches of industry in which production can be rapidly expanded (manufacture proper, mining, etc.), climbing prices give rise to sudden expansion soon followed by collapse. The same effect is produced in the labour-market, attracting great numbers of the latent relative surplus-population, and even of the employed labourers, to the new lines of business. In general such large-scale undertakings as railways withdraw a definite quantity of labour-power from the labour-market, which can come only from such lines of business as agriculture, etc., where only strong lads are needed. This still continues even after the new enterprises have become established lines of business and the migratory working-class needed for them has already been formed, as for instance in the case of temporary rise above the average in the scale of railway construction. A portion of the reserve army of labourers, which keep wages down, is absorbed. A general rise in wages ensues, even in the hitherto well employed sections of the labour-market. This lasts until the inevitable crash again releases the reserve army of labour and wages are once more depressed to their minimum, and lower.
