spoken · the reader's introductionWelcome to the seventh video in the series, The Character of the Communist Mode of Production According to Marx and Engels. Today we’ll be going over the 1857 piece by Marx, the Grundrisse, Notebook Seven, the Chapter on Capital. And as you might recall from the previous recording, the Grundrisse is Marx’s notebooks in preparation for writing Capital, which we will be going over some chapters of in the next pieces. I’ve subtitled this “Social Control by the General Intellect Brings Freedom.”

In this text, Marx talks about the character of mankind's relationship with technology under Capitalism and thereby under Communism, the content of real wealth, the bringing of social life under the control of the general intellect and the end of alienation on these terms, the development of the productive forces under Communism, and the growth of culture as Communism develops. The Command of Labor, the end of poverty, the liberation of man from abstract time and value, and the superiority of Communism as a way to do large-scale production is explained, along with an aspect of the development of the Metabolic Rift under Capitalism.

Within Capitalism, the worker is slaved to the Dead Labor of the machines that he has built, and is in competition with his own product which is developed under conditions of Capitalist Industrialization, which presses science into the service of Capital and converts invention into a business under the ownership of the Bourgeoisie, thus intensifying the division of labor and pressing labor into Capital as a mechanical and replicable machine that devalues labor itself.

This makes it so the worker hates and fears technological development, and is alienated and enslaved by it, rather than liberated. In Communism, it is the opposite. Science is in the service of humanity as a whole, development of the productive forces liberates mankind, and the whole of society becomes scientific. It ends the division of labor, rather than reinforcing it. Since large industry causes wealth creation to be about efficiency of labor time rather than total labor time and laborers, as the productive forces develop under socialism, labor ceases in production process and instead human activity becomes that of a watchman and regulator to the process, wherein the worker inserts a process of nature (e.g. a chemical reaction) into an industrial process and becomes a master of that nature, letting it take its course So as an example, (I do not need to know how electricity or wiring works to flick a light switch and have it work). Right? This is scientific society, but the whole of society plays under this relation. This appropriation of general productive power, rather than direct human labor, in the form of understanding of nature and mastery of it learned through society, becomes a new form of foundation for wealth for a new social individual: a scientist who ceases division of mental and physical labor.

To this new scientific individual, within a scientific society, theft of labor time is extremely inefficient and it only fetters the development of large scale industry; with labor ceasing in direct form, labor time ceases and stops being a measure. Hence, with the end of the division of labor and the basis of labor as the measure of value, the Labor Theory of Value ceases and Value ceases measuring Use Value. Surplus Labor ceases to be the basis for the development of general wealth, and as such any Malthusian notions lose any and all basis.

In this scientific society, with wealth no longer based on the exploitation of surplus labor time, we can instead focus on maximizing surplus labor. Thus, Communism will create large amounts of leisure time- that is, time outside of necessary labor time for society generally to reproduce itself. This free time will allow for the development of individuals' full productive forces, which further increases the productive efficiency of society; under Capitalism, leisure time is seen as an aberration, laziness and idleness, a crime for not producing surplus value. Workers are reduced to machinery and forced into conditions of hopeless degradation in order to maintain power over them and their labor: health, comfort and leisure are robbed from us and the Division of Labor and goal of surplus extraction leads to monotonous and lengthy labor, creating bored and unthinking workers which produces bad habits and poor intellectual pursuits: physical, moral, and intellectual amusements are perverted or barred from the worker, as the worker lacks time or access to anything higher, all of which is produced by the bourgeoisie, for the bourgeoisie, as their command of labor allows them to control civilization and its products: art, society, and so on.

It is this command over labor, through private property, which produces poverty: "What divides the rich from the poorer is not the ownership of land or of money, but rather the command of labour", and as time is the basis of labor, it is also command over the lifespans and time of the vast majority of humanity.

Under Communism, however, leisure ceases being antithetical. Labor time becomes measured by the needs of the social individual, while at the same time, the development of the power of social production is so rapid and expansive that, despite increasing needs of the social individual, production outstrips it. Hence, leisure time becomes synonymous with wealth, and the previous character of value, labor time, necessarily means that wealth is founded upon poverty which degrades the individual to that of being a mere worker subsumed under labor. Under Communism, civilization commands its own labor and art, society, etc., and so culture is produced by civilization, for civilization.

Communist society, therefore, allows the free development of individuals (rather than aiming to reduce labor time to maximize surplus labor) and allows for a general reduction of the necessary labor of society to a minimum, thus allowing maximal artistic, scientific, etc development of individuals as all have full free time, access to all they need for full development, etc.

With:
1. the end of the division between mental and physical labor
2. the full development of all individuals
3. the full recognition of labor
4. the end of alienation of control over society to an alien mediator and alien unconscious
5. and the pressing of all social production to direct character and in the service of society rather than capital
the General Intellect will thus be fully developed and fully applied to the real concerns of society without any of the aforementioned fetters

This General Intellect is the objectified social knowledge of humanity, which in turn determines society's fixed capital: technological expertise combined with social knowledge as machinery increases in importance in social organization. As society develops, in order to better and more efficiently engage in production, the General Intellect understands that the productive forces must be more and more centralized, as only highly centralized and organized production can maximize the production of annual wealth that can be consumed by all.

Thus, all production under Communism is maximally centralized.

The full development of the productive forces and individual producers, and the full association of the individual producers, will create the need for society to transcend itself and reach a new form: the Higher Phase of Communism.

In addition to the maximization of efficiency of production in terms of centralization and organization, unlike in Bourgeois society wherein turnover, reproduction of fixed capital requires labor to be used on any fixed capital in stock so as to pay for itself and its interest (thus requiring labor be done on fixed capital even if products of that labor cease being consumed, in the hopes of drawing blood from stone), Communist society has no such requirements: productive forces can be moved rapidly according to changes in social desire, and there is no required anticipation of future fruits of labor requiring labor be done uselessly.

Unlike Capitalism, which does not care what it produces or even if it does produce, but only cares about profit, Communism ONLY cares for what Use Values it produces.

Unlike Capitalism, which would rather get away with NOT developing machinery, while still deriving as much profit, Communism has DIRECT interest in developing the productive forces, as it produces use values and reduces labor time.

Society must reproduce itself materially in particular ways: Capitalism's mode of production requires that the Proletarian exchange his product (labor-power) for the conditions of production (wage goods) so as to do it all over the next day, and transform product into money to reproduce capital, which is in turn necessary to produce surplus value. It must contain the reproduction of use values which realize the Capitalist mode of production, or it expends the human labor and land it relies upon. Thus, Capital transforms labor and nature into its own form, even when that is impossible to maintain: the bourgeois drive for increased production and consumption makes it so it is wholly unnatural and out of sync with the conditions of its reproduction: natural forces; this in turn reduces long-term efficiency and use of nature. Capital has no choice but to have systems of circulation despite not producing surplus value, because it requires them for its realization: thus, production is subordinate in every way to that alien process of realization: exchange.

Under Communism, however, the conditions for reproduction are different. Production is assumed to be social, there is no exchange or process of transformation- there are no fetters on production and consumption based on such, and both are direct. It is in sync with natural forces and humanity. And this is how the metabolic rift is repaired, and we get out of our continuing, worsening ecological crisis that has developed and continues to develop completely unabated by the capitalist powers of the world.

In machinery, the appropriation of living labour by capital achieves a direct reality in this respect as well: It is, firstly, the analysis and application of mechanical and chemical laws, arising directly out of science, which enables the machine to perform the same labour as that previously performed by the worker. However, the development of machinery along this path occurs only when large industry has already reached a higher stage, and all the sciences have been pressed into the service of capital; and when, secondly, the available machinery itself already provides great capabilities. Invention then becomes a business, and the application of science to direct production itself becomes a prospect which determines and solicits it. But this is not the road along which machinery, by and large, arose, and even less the road on which it progresses in detail. This road is, rather, dissection [Analyse] – through the division of labour, which gradually transforms the workers’ operations into more and more mechanical ones, so that at a certain point a mechanism can step into their places. (See under economy of power.) Thus, the specific mode of working here appears directly as becoming transferred from the worker to capital in the form of the machine, and his own labour capacity devalued thereby. Hence the workers’ struggle against machinery. What was the living worker’s activity becomes the activity of the machine. Thus the appropriation of labour by capital confronts the worker in a coarsely sensuous form; capital absorbs labour into itself – ‘as though its body were by love possessed’.

  1. The enslavement of the worker to the machinery, the degradation of labor, the competition between living and dead labor (fixed capital) within Capitalism
  2. The distinction between Capitalist Industrialization and Socialist Industrialization. Science is pressed into the service of Capital, Invention becomes a business, and the application of science to direct production becomes something decided by the bourgeoisie
  3. Machinery historically developed in the support of the division of labor, and in this pressing of labor to Capital, rather than liberating work instead transforms the worker into a mechanical and replaceable machine while devaluing labor itself
  4. This results in labor being against technological development, rather than for it

But to the degree that large industry develops, the creation of real wealth comes to depend less on labour time and on the amount of labour employed than on the power of the agencies set in motion during labour time, whose ‘powerful effectiveness’ is itself in turn out of all proportion to the direct labour time spent on their production, but depends rather on the general state of science and on the progress of technology, or the application of this science to production. (The development of this science, especially natural science, and all others with the latter, is itself in turn related to the development of material production.) Agriculture, e.g., becomes merely the application of the science of material metabolism, its regulation for the greatest advantage of the entire body of society. Real wealth manifests itself, rather – and large industry reveals this – in the monstrous disproportion between the labour time applied, and its product, as well as in the qualitative imbalance between labour, reduced to a pure abstraction, and the power of the production process it superintends. Labour no longer appears so much to be included within the production process; rather, the human being comes to relate more as watchman and regulator to the production process itself. (What holds for machinery holds likewise for the combination of human activities and the development of human intercourse.) No longer does the worker insert a modified natural thing [Naturgegenstand] as middle link between the object [Objekt] and himself; rather, he inserts the process of nature, transformed into an industrial process, as a means between himself and inorganic nature, mastering it. He steps to the side of the production process instead of being its chief actor. In this transformation, it is neither the direct human labour he himself performs, nor the time during which he works, but rather the appropriation of his own general productive power, his understanding of nature and his mastery over it by virtue of his presence as a social body – it is, in a word, the development of the social individual which appears as the great foundation-stone of production and of wealth. The theft of alien labour time, on which the present wealth is based, appears a miserable foundation in face of this new one, created by large-scale industry itself. As soon as labour in the direct form has ceased to be the great well-spring of wealth, labour time ceases and must cease to be its measure, and hence exchange value [must cease to be the measure] of use value. The surplus labour of the mass has ceased to be the condition for the development of general wealth, just as the non-labour of the few, for the development of the general powers of the human head. With that, production based on exchange value breaks down, and the direct, material production process is stripped of the form of penury and antithesis. The free development of individualities, and hence not the reduction of necessary labour time so as to posit surplus labour, but rather the general reduction of the necessary labour of society to a minimum, which then corresponds to the artistic, scientific etc. development of the individuals in the time set free, and with the means created, for all of them.

  1. As large industry develops, wealth creation ceases being about total labor time and laborers, and instead the efficiency of that labor time. This depends on the general state of science and technological progress, which is predicated on the development of material production
  2. Agriculture becomes the application of the science of material metabolism which must be regulated in all societies for maximal use
  3. Over time, labor ceases in production process and instead human activity becomes that of a watchman and regulator to the process
  4. The worker inserts a process of nature transformed into an industrial process and becomes a master of that nature, letting nature take its course. This appropriation of general productive power, rather than direct human labor, in the form of understanding of nature and mastery of it learned through society, becomes a new form of foundation for wealth for a new social individual: a scientist who ceases division of mental and physical labor
  5. To this new scientific individual, theft of labor time is extremely inefficient, and only fetters large scale industry
  6. When labor ceases in direct form, labor time ceases and stops being a measure. Value ceases measuring Use Value
  7. Surplus Labor ceases to be the basis for the development of general wealth: Malthusianism ceases in any sense of the word
  8. As a result, the free development of individuals (rather than aiming to reduce labor time to maximize surplus labor) allows for a general reduction of the necessary labor of society to a minimum, thus allowing maximal artistic, scientific, etc development of individuals as all have full free time, access to all they need for full development, etc

Nature builds no machines, no locomotives, railways, electric telegraphs, self-acting mules etc. These are products of human industry; natural material transformed into organs of the human will over nature, or of human participation in nature. They are organs of the human brain, created by the human hand; the power of knowledge, objectified. The development of fixed capital indicates to what degree general social knowledge has become a direct force of production, and to what degree, hence, the conditions of the process of social life itself have come under the control of the general intellect and been transformed in accordance with it. To what degree the powers of social production have been produced, not only in the form of knowledge, but also as immediate organs of social practice, of the real life process.

  1. Humans convert nature into machines. These machines are organs of the human brain. They are social knowledge that is objectified. Thus, the development of society's fixed capital is dependent on this social knowledge. What machines are produced are determined by the general intellect: technological expertise combined with social knowledge as machinery increases in importance in social organization.
  2. With the end of the division between mental and physical labor, the full development of all individuals, the full recognition of labor, the end of alienation of control over society to an alien mediator and alien unconscious, the pressing of all social production to direct character and in the service of society rather than capital, the General Intellect will thus be fully developed and fully applied to the real concerns of society without any of the aforementioned fetters

The creation of a large quantity of disposable time apart from necessary labour time for society generally and each of its members (i.e. room for the development of the individuals’ full productive forces, hence those of society also), this creation of not-labour time appears in the stage of capital, as of all earlier ones, as not-labour time, free time, for a few. What capital adds is that it increases the surplus labour time of the mass by all the means of art and science, because its wealth consists directly in the appropriation of surplus labour time; since value directly its purpose, not use value. It is thus, despite itself, instrumental in creating the means of social disposable time, in order to reduce labour time for the whole society to a diminishing minimum, and thus to free everyone’s time for their own development. But its tendency always, on the one side, to create disposable time, on the other, to convert it into surplus labour. If it succeeds too well at the first, then it suffers from surplus production, and then necessary labour is interrupted, because no surplus labour can be realized by capital. The more this contradiction develops, the more does it become evident that the growth of the forces of production can no longer be bound up with the appropriation of alien labour, but that the mass of workers must themselves appropriate their own surplus labour. Once they have done so – and disposable time thereby ceases to have an antithetical existence – then, on one side, necessary labour time will be measured by the needs of the social individual, and, on the other, the development of the power of social production will grow so rapidly that, even though production is now calculated for the wealth of all, disposable time will grow for all. For real wealth is the developed productive power of all individuals. The measure of wealth is then not any longer, in any way, labour time, but rather disposable time. Labour time as the measure of value posits wealth itself as founded on poverty, and disposable time as existing in and because of the antithesis to surplus labour time; or, the positing of an individual’s entire time as labour time, and his degradation therefore to mere worker, subsumption under labour. The most developed machinery thus forces the worker to work longer than the savage does, or than he himself did with the simplest, crudest tools.

  1. Since wealth is no longer based on exploitation of surplus labor time, we can instead focus on minimizing surplus labor. Communism will thus create large amounts of leisure time (time outside of necessary labor time for society generally)
  2. This free time will allow for the development of individuals' full productive forces, which further increases the productivity efficiency of society
  3. Under Capitalism, leisure time is seen as an aberration, as laziness and idleness, since it is not producing surplus value. However, under Communism, it ceases being antithetical
  4. This results in labor time being measured by the needs of the social individual, while at the same time the development of the power of social production is so rapid and expansive that, despite increasing needs of the social individual, production outstrips it
  5. Wealth becomes leisure time, then, under Communism
  6. Labor Time being the measure of value necessarily means that wealth is founded on poverty, and degrades the individual to that of being a mere worker subsumed under labor

Without great capitals no great establishments can be founded; men cannot be brought to understand the practicability of effecting new combinations, in order to ensure a superior character to all and the production of more annual wealth than can be consumed by all; and that wealth, too, should be of a higher kind than that hitherto generally produced.’ (loc. cit. 56, 57.) ‘It is this new chemical and mechanical manufacturing system which now expands human abilities, and prepares men to understand and to adopt other principles and practices, and thus to effect the most beneficial change in affairs which the world has yet known. And it is this new manufacturing system which now creates the necessity for another and higher classification of society.’ (loc. cit. 58.))

  1. As society develops, in order to better and more efficiently engage in production, the productive forces must be more and more centralized
  2. Since only centralized, organized production can maximize the production of annual wealth that can be consumed by all, Communism thus must have production maximally centralized
  3. The full development of the productive forces and individual producers, and the full association of the individual producers, will create a higher classification of society

His servants, as they are called, his slaves in fact, are reduced to the most hopeless degradation; the majority robbed of health, of domestic comfort, of the leisure and healthy open-air pleasures of earlier days. Through excessive exhaustion of their powers, brought about by lengthy, drawn-out monotonous occupations, they are seduced into habits of intemperance, and made unfit for thinking or reflection. They can have no physical, intellectual or moral amusements other than of the worst sort; all real pleasures of life are far distant from them. The life which a very large part of the workers lead under the present system is, in a word, not worth having.

  1. Under Capitalism, workers are reduced to machinery and forced into conditions of hopeless degradation in order to maintain power over them
  2. We are robbed of health, comfort, and leisure. The Division of Labor and the goal of surplus labor extraction leads to monotonous and lengthy labor, creating bored and unthinking workers
  3. Thus, all sorts of bad habits and intellectual pursuits develop, and physical, intellectual and moral amusements become perverted or barred from us because there lacks time or access to anything higher

On the basis of the aspect developed so far, no other relations exist. The commodity which is ejected as such from the circulation of capital loses its character as value and fulfills the role of use value for consumption, as distinct from fulfilling it for production.

  1. Commodities removed from the Exchange relation lose their Commodity form, becoming use values for consumption

In another respect, since fixed capital is exchanged only to the extent it enters as value into circulating capital, since it is, thus, realized only in part during the year, it presupposes only a partial counter-value, i.e. only the partial production of this counter-value during the course of the year. It is paid for only in proportion to its wear. This much clear, then, which already follows from the difference introduced by fixed capital into the industrial cycle, namely that it engages the production of subsequent years, and, just as it contributes to the creation of a large revenue, it anticipates further labour as a counter-value. The anticipation of future fruits of labour is therefore in no way a consequence of the state debt etc., in short, not an invention of the credit system. It has its roots in the specific mode of realization, mode of turnover, mode of reproduction of fixed capital

  1. Bourgeois mode of realizations, turnover, reproduction of fixed capital requires labor to be used on any fixed capital in stock, else it will not pay for itself/interest. Even after it ceases to be consumed, it is still labored upon in the hopes of drawing blood from stone
  2. Communist society has no such requirements, and as such can move productive forces rapidly according to changes in social desire. It has no such required anticipation of future fruits of labor subsumed by those relations

We have seen that only part of the fixed capital returns in the time determined by circulating capital, which serves as the unit of its turnovers because it is the natural unit for the reproduction of the greatest part of food products and raw materials, just as, and because, it appears as the natural epoch in the life process (cosmic process) of the earth. This unit is the year, whose bourgeois calculation deviates more or less, but insignificantly, from its natural magnitude. The more the material presence of fixed capital corresponds to its concept, the more adequate its material mode of existence is, the more does its turnover time span a cycle of years.

  1. The bourgeois drive for increased production and consumption makes it it so it is wholly unnatural and out of sync with the conditions of its own reproduction: natural forces
  2. This reduces long-term efficiency and use of nature

‘Our zone requires labour for the satisfaction of needs, and therefore at least one part of society must always tirelessly labour; others labour in the arts etc., and some, who do not work, still have the products of diligence at their disposal. For this, these proprietors have only civilization and order to thank; they are purely the creatures of civilized institutions. For these have recognized that one can also obtain the fruits of labour through ways other than labour; the men of independent fortune owe their wealth almost entirely to the labour of others, not to their own ability, which is not at all better. What divides the rich from the poorer is not the ownership of land or of money, but rather the command of labour.’

  1. Command of Labor allows for control over civilization and its products: art, society, and so on. However, because it is civilization that produces these, only civilization can lay claim to them.
  2. In Communism, civilization commands its own labor and art, society, etc are produced by civilization, for civilization
  3. Poverty, in truth, emerges out of Private Property, not out of any nature character. Those who owe their wealth to the labor of others- that is, through the command of labor- produce poverty. "What divides the rich from the poorer is not the ownership of land or of money, but rather the command of labour."
  4. As time is also the basis of labor, it is also command over the lifespans and time of the vast majority of humanity

The less the value of the fixed capital in relation to its effectiveness, the more does it correspond to its purpose. All unnecessary fixed capital appears as faux frais de production, like all unnecessary circulation costs. If capital could possess the machinery without employing labour for the purpose, then it would raise the productive power of labour and diminish necessary labour without having to buy labour. The value of the fixed capital is therefore never an end in itself in the production of capital.

  1. Capitalism does not care whether it actually produces, only about profit: it would produce nothing if it could get away with it. Communism, on the other hand, cares only for the use values it produces
  2. If Capitalism could get away with NOT developing machinery and still get as much profit, it would do so as well. Communism has direct interest in developing the productive forces, as it produces use values and reduces labor time
  3. If Capitalism could avoid employing labor at all and instead use only machines, it would also do so in a heartbeat. For Capital, the value of fixed capital is never an end in itself in its production

The production process, as containing within itself the conditions of its renewal, is a reproduction process whose speed is determined by various relations developed above, which all arise from differences of circulation. The reproduction of capital also contains the reproduction of the use values in which it is realized – or the constant renewal and reproduction by human labour of the use values which enter human consumption and are themselves perishable. The change of substance and of form subordinated to human need through human labour appears from the viewpoint of capital as its own reproduction. It is at bottom the constant reproduction of labour itself. ‘Capital values perpetuate themselves by reproduction: the products which compose a capital are consumed just like any others; but their value, at the same time as it is destroyed by consumption, is reproduced in other materials or in the same one.’ (Say, 14.) [19] Exchange and a system of exchanges, and, included in that, the transformation into money as independent value, appears as condition and barrier for the reproduction of capital. With capital, production itself is on all sides subordinate to exchange. These exchange operations, circulation as such, produce no surplus value, but are conditions for its realization. They are conditions of the production of capital itself, in so far as its form as capital is posited only to the extent that it passes through them. The reproduction of capital is at the same time the production of specific formal conditions; of specific modes of relationship in which personified objectified labour is posited. Circulation is thus not merely the exchange of the product for the conditions of production – i.e. of produced wheat, e.g., for seed, new labour etc. The worker must exchange his product for the conditions of production, so as to begin anew, in every form of production. The peasant producing for immediate consumption also transforms part of the product into seed, instrument of labour, beasts of burden, fertilizer etc., and begins his labour anew. The transformation into money is necessary for the reproduction of capital as such, and its reproduction is necessarily the production of surplus value. * Although labour must merely maintain the value of what we earlier called constant capital in one production process, it must constantly reproduce it in another, since what appears as presupposition of material and instrument in one production process is product in the other, and this renewal, reproduction, must constantly proceed simultaneously.

  1. Society must reproduce itself materially, capitalism reproduces itself in particular ways, Communism will reproduce itself materially in another way

a. The Proletarian must exchange his product (labor-power) for the conditions of production (wage goods) so as to do it all over the next day;

    b. The Peasant (producing for immediate consumption) must transform part of their product into seed, instrument of labor, beast of burden, fertilizer, etc so as to do it all over the next day

      c. The Capitalist mode of production must transform product into money to reproduce capital, which is necessary to produce surplus value

      1. Capitalism's reproduction must necessarily contain the reproduction of use values which realize the Capitalist mode of production, else it expends the human labor and land on which it relies upon
      2. Capital thus transforms labor and nature into its own form
      3. Exchange and other circulation within Capitalism, are not production itself, but are a condition and barrier for the reproduction of capital. They do not produce surplus value, but are the condition for its realization and capital only forms when it passes through exchange
      4. In Capitalism, production is fully subordinate in every way to exchange
