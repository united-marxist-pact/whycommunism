spoken · the reader's introductionWelcome to the second video in this series: the character of the Communist Mode of Production According to Marx and Engels. Today we’ll be going over the 1844 piece by Marx: Comments on James Mill, Éléments d’économie politique. I’ve subtitled this: Communism Achieves Humanization, Ends Alienation and Oppression. Let’s talk about what that means, with my summary of this text.

This section of Marx's larger Paris Notebooks addresses the bourgeois economist James Mill, before addressing a "larger, more tangential thought", namely developing his what I would term (Post)-Humanist conception, discussing Alienation, the Division of Labor, Human Nature, what he sees as the potential of Communism as the associated mode of production which is the True Human Community that socializes mankind.

To Marx, human nature is synonymous with the true community of men which is produced by the essential nature of each individual with their activity, life, spirit and wealth. It arises out of the need and egoism of individuals produced by their essential nature as people exchange activity and production’s products with one another, and not through changing one’s individual perception of their condition: it is materially and socially achieved. This puts him immediately at odds with the entirety of Idealist philosophers, who saw the ego and spirit as achieving humanization despite material conditions as individuals.

In order for this true human community to arise, what fundamentally must end is Alienation, or the estrangement of mankind from this true community. Alienation makes the community we have, the Gesselschaft (historical society) of the given era, impossible to reach for humanity because our human nature as social producers within reality has been disrupted by the Capitalist Mode of Production (and in previous Modes of Production). It is not life-fulfilling or spiritually, intellectually, or socially stimulating, and being involved in the production process does not produce wealth for yourself- that is to say, increase in leisure time or real use values. Instead, it produces Exchange Values for someone else. In fact, production in Capitalism abolishes the self, with the more one labors the less they are able to live. The products the producer creates rule over the creator, and the wealth of one person becomes the poverty of another: other human individuals become threats to be competed with or inessential, with humans measured and quantified in accordance with principles external to human society. Rather, it is something else that does the deciding about what has value and what does not: the Real God that is Capital. All social power is alienated onto this social process out of the hands of the people subjected to that process.

Capitalism itself produces this Alienation through its basis in Private Property, which necessitates that someone can lose their property through Exchange. Thus, all property is alienated, equivocated, and extracted from its own physical character to become a reflection of all other commodities. All products of human labor, and nature itself upon being rendered into useable form by human labor, lose their personal character, unique existence, and unity with themselves in exchange for unity with all other Commodities. Because Commodities rule over producers, and Commodities are reflected in all other Commodities, the producer becomes ruled by all Commodities. This process is and results in Commodity Fetishism.

Private Property leads to production no longer being for the actual product, but rather for the ability of it to be exchanged. Thus, the social labor that is put into producing it becomes alienated and externalized. Through our own commodities, labor becomes alienated in the act of production and consumption. In addition, Exchange of products of human labor in turn produces mutual exchange of the activity of production. This creates a new form of a social Division of Labor, which converts man from a universal and complete human, into one who is shaped into a tool for a particular mental and physical kind of labor in the hands of the Capitalist and the Market, discarded when no longer fit.

GESELLSCHAFTGEMEINSCHAFT

Communism negates this, bringing the Gesellschaft into a new form, from a society of individualism and competition along with impersonal relations driven by economic self-interest, into the Gemeinschaft, the True Human Community of cooperation, solidarity and shared purpose wherein Labor, which is the manifestation of Human Nature, is synonymous with passion and wealth for the individual in and through society, resulting in one in which there is true, total humanization. Alienation is abolished, regenerating the conditions of social metabolism, as property is held in common by all. In this new society, no one loses personal command over anything they produce while also having access to all products of society through the gennoschaftlicher Reichtum, or Common Wealth. Excuse me for my German. All products of society lose their dual character and become unique existences and, through the abolition of Exchange, the abolition of the social Division of Labor finally becomes possible. The abolition of Exchange in turn requires the abolition of Private Property which creates a society of Associated Producers.

A Mode of Production that is based on the Free and Equal Associated Producers is, according to Marx, necessarily one which affirms all parties in the production process while objectifying each other their individualities in a sensuous manner, allowing the producer(s) to recognize the product's use in the hands of the user, thus understanding the social nature of their labor. This allows them to fulfill themselves socially, intellectually and spiritually. In turn, the product is known to the consumer, and the consumer is known to the producer. This also requires that the Associated Producers have direct relations with one another, without any mediator between them such as Commodities or Money or Exchange. Under these circumstances, Labor becomes personal and concrete, rather than abstract and homogenized through Exchange.

Production in this society is for what is considered useful for the True Community, which is solely for

  1. Life-Activity (in this relation, Passion) which fulfills an individual spiritually, socially, intellectually, or physically. This production is done socially and for personal and specific reason
  2. Wealth for society and the self through society in the form of Leisure Time and Use Values, for the Common Wealth. It is reciprocal; the individual produces for society, and society in turn produces for the individual.

In such a society, Man and Man, Man and Nature, and Man and Community are all unified in relation to one another, and in and through this unification, the individual reaches full development. Thus, Labor becomes the free manifestation of life and as such, life’s prime want, through the end of alienation. It ceases to be something avoided and considered solely for subsistence by way of subjecting oneself to another.

As such, Communism regenerates the social metabolism through the ending of alienation, which brings back the true community of mankind. It does this by the abolition of Private Property, which results in the end of Exchange, Division of Labor, and unifies Man with his conditions, with all other producers, and the products of society. Labor then becomes life’s prime want, as it becomes passion itself in and through society. All individuals thus reach full development to their highest form.

QUOTATION EXTRACTS · THE SILVERED ORIGINALS

Exchange, both of human activity within production itself and of human product against one another, is equivalent to species-activity and species-spirit, the real, conscious and true mode of existence of which is social activity and social enjoyment. Since human nature is the true community of men, by manifesting their nature men create, produce, the human community, the social entity, which is no abstract universal power opposed to the single individual, but is the essential nature of each individual, his own activity, his own life, his own spirit, his own wealth. Hence this true community does not come into being through reflection, it appears owing to the need and egoism of individuals, i.e., it is produced directly by their life activity itself. It does not depend on man whether this community exists or not; but as long as man does not recognise himself as man, and therefore has not organised the world in a human way, this community appears in the form of estrangement, because its subject, man, is a being estranged from himself. Men, not as an abstraction, but as real, living, particular individuals, are this entity. Hence, as they are, so is this entity itself. To say that man is estranged from himself, therefore, is the same thing as saying that the society of this estranged man is a caricature of his real community, of his true species-life, that his activity therefore appears to him as a torment, his own creation as an alien power, his wealth as poverty, the essential bond linking him with other men as an unessential bond, and separation from his fellow men, on the other hand, as his true mode of existence, his life as a sacrifice of his life, the realisation of his nature as making his life unreal, his production as the production of his nullity, his power over an object as the power of the object over him, and he himself, the lord of his creation, as the servant of this creation.

The community of men, or the manifestation of the nature of men, their mutual complementing the result of which is species-life, truly human life – this community is conceived by political economy in the form of exchange and trade. Society, says Destutt de Tracy, is a series of mutual exchanges. It is precisely this process of mutual integration. Society, says Adam Smith, is a commercial society. Each of its members is a merchant.

It is seen that political economy defines the estranged form of social intercourse as the essential and original form corresponding to man's nature.

  1. Exchange between humans is the fullest of social activity and enjoyment
  2. Humanity manifesting their nature to create produces human community
  3. Human community is not an abstract, universal power opposed to the individual, but is the "essential nature of each individual, his own activity, life, spirit, wealth"
  4. True Community (Human Nature) doesn't come through reflection, but rather arises out of the need and egoism of individuals produced by their life activity
  5. Through Alienation of man from himself, he is alienated from his community, and vice versa. The community becomes alien, production becomes torment and self-abolition and those products rule over him, wealth (leisure time, use value) becomes poverty, others become threats or unessential,
  6. Currently, political-economy perceives Community as synonymous with mutual exchange and trade; in truth, though, this definition is the negation of community

Through the reciprocal alienation or estrangement of private property, private property, itself falls into the category of alienated private property.[2] For, in the first place, it has ceased to be the product of the labour of its owner, his exclusive, distinctive personality. For he has alienated it, it has moved away from the owner whose product it was and has acquired a personal significance for someone whose product it is not. It has lost its personal significance for the owner. Secondly, it has been brought into relation with another private property, and placed on a par with the latter. Its place has been taken by a private property of a different kind, just as it itself takes the place of a private property of a different kind. On both sides, therefore, private property appears as the representative of a different kind of private property, as the equivalent of a different natural product, and both sides are related to each other in such a way that each represents the mode of existence of the other, and both relate to each other as substitutes for themselves and the other. Hence the mode of existence of private property as such has become that of a substitute, of an equivalent. Instead of its immediate unity with itself, it exists now only as a relation to something else. Its mode of existence as an equivalent is no longer its specific mode of existence. It has thus become a value, and immediately an exchange-value. Its mode of existence as value is an alienated designation of itself, different from its immediate existence, external to its specific nature, a merely relative mode of existence of this.

  1. Private Property assumes that property can leave the hands of its owner. Thus, private property is necessarily alienated, ceasing to be the product of the labor of its owner imbued with their own human character
  2. The act of exchange loses the quality of the product its personal significance
  3. Exchange also necessitates it be put into such a relation with another unit of private property, thus alienating that as well; they cease to have their own unique properties and instead become eachother, equated through abstraction. It loses its immediate unity with itself and exists only as a relation to something else
  4. Its transformation from a unique mode of existence into an equivalent one causes it to become a Value, and, in dual character, an Exchange-Value
  5. This Value is an alienated, external, relative character to the product itself. Mankind becomes alienated in a double way from their products

Just as the mutual exchange of the products of human activity appears as barter, as trade, so the mutual completion and exchange of the activity itself appears as division of labour, which turns man as far as possible into an abstract being, a machine tool, etc., and transforms him into a spiritual and physical monster.

It is precisely the unity of human labour that is regarded merely as division of labour, because social nature only comes into existence as its opposite, in the form of estrangement. Division of labour increases with civilisation.

Within the presupposition of division of labour, the product, the material of private property, acquires for the individual more and more the significance of an equivalent, and as he no longer exchanges only his surplus, and the object of his production can be simply a matter of indifference to him, so too he no longer exchanges his product for something directly needed by him. The equivalent comes into existence as an equivalent in money, which is now the immediate result of labour to gain a living and the medium of exchange (see above).

  1. Mutual exchange of products ultimately means mutual exchange of the activity of production. Thus, a division of labor develops. This division of labor turns man into an abstract tool, reshaping him physically and mentally within the limits of their particular labor
  2. Alienation involves the rending down of labor into merely working for subsistence, and not to produce things that you want

Let us suppose that we had carried out production as human beings. Each of us would have in two ways affirmed himself and the other person.

1) In my production I would have objectified my individuality, its specific character, and therefore enjoyed not only an individual manifestation of my life during the activity, but also when looking at the object I would have the individual pleasure of knowing my personality to be objective, visible to the senses and hence a power beyond all doubt.

2) In your enjoyment or use of my product I would have the direct enjoyment both of being conscious of having satisfied a human need by my work, that is, of having objectified man's essential nature, and of having thus created an object corresponding to the need of another man's essential nature.

3) I would have been for you the mediator between you and the species, and therefore would become recognised and felt by you yourself as a completion of your own essential nature and as a necessary part of yourself, and consequently would know myself to be confirmed both in your thought and your love.

4) In the individual expression of my life I would have directly created your expression of your life, and therefore in my individual activity I would have directly confirmed and realised my true nature, my human nature, my communal nature.

Our products would be so many mirrors in which we saw reflected our essential nature.

This relationship would moreover be reciprocal; what occurs on my side has also to occur on yours.

  1. Associated Production is an affirmation of all parties
  2. It objectifies the producer's individuality in a sensuous manner
  3. The producer recognizes the product's use in the hands of the user: a job well done for a happy customer
  4. It would have no mediator between the associated producers: it would be direct
  5. The production is seen as part of the social whole and useful, and thereby the associated producers know their actions were fulfilling themselves in and through society

Let us review the various factors as seen in our supposition:

My work would be a free manifestation of life, hence an enjoyment of life. Presupposing private property, my work is an alienation of life, for I work in order to live, in order to obtain for myself the means of life. My work is not my life.

Secondly, the specific nature of my individuality, therefore, would be affirmed in my labour, since the latter would be an affirmation of my individual life. Labour therefore would be true, active property. Presupposing private property, my individuality is alienated to such a degree that this activity is instead hateful to me, a torment, and rather the semblance of an activity. Hence, too, it is only a forced activity and one imposed on me only through an external fortuitous need, not through an inner, essential one.

My labour can appear in my object only as what it is. It cannot appear as something which by its nature it is not. Hence it appears only as the expression of my loss of self and of my powerlessness that is objective, sensuously perceptible, obvious and therefore put beyond all doubt.

    In a society without alienation:

    1. My labor is personal and concrete, rather than abstract and without personal character
    2. The product of my labor and the results of it are known to me; the need another has for it is recognized with the product being in my "line of sight"
    3. It is a mutual gain for production and consumption. The producer is known to the consumer and the consumer is known to the producer
    4. Production is social in character, and done as personal rather than empty of reason, and it is reciprocal
    5. Labor is a free manifestation of life; it becomes life's prime want rather than something to be avoided and considered solely for subsistence by way of subjecting oneself to another
    6. Labor is unified with itself, with the product of that labor being used for its intended purpose, its use. Not for exchange.
    7. Man and Man, Man and Nature, Man and Community, are all unified in relation to one another, with the individual maximized
