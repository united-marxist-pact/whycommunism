spoken · the reader's introductionWelcome to the eighth video in the series, The Character of the Communist Mode of Production According to Marx and Engels. Today we’ll be going over the 1867 piece by Marx, Capital, Volume One, Part One, Commodities and Money, Chapter One, Commodities. I have subtitled this, Communism is Natural, Spontaneous, and Direct Social Organization.

Communism is the Negation of the Negation of Capitalism and Classed Society as a whole; it is not simply a direct and base rejection of it, but rather a fulfillment of the varying contradictory forces within Capitalism coming to a head and carrying out an internal revolution and transformation which does not do away with the developments of previous classed society, but rather transforms them and thus overcomes and preserves all previous society in new form. One cannot understand Communism without understanding Capitalism, the origin of the premises of Communism’s existence. All that makes up Capitalism is negated, and in turn, that negated state is washed away in the same way that the true negation of religion is not atheism, but simply not even having a conception of religion. Therefore, the character of Communism can be found through the investigation of Capitalism, with the understanding that all that Capital is- a generalized commodity producing society- is directly reversed, transformed, and reconstituted, and then liberated from those social contradictions.

In Capitalism, as a mode of production characterized by general commodity production, production becomes specifically for the purpose of the products being exchanged, resulting in the value being taken into account before and during production. This causes the labor content to have a two-fold character. The labor must produce a socially useful product as a use-value (its social utility, as something a given society desires for whatever reason) and have an exchange-value (equalized to all other kinds of labor, abstract from any inequalities principally via reduction to common denominator as human labor in the abstract; that is, labor-power). All products therefore, must be useful for others, and the labor of anyone is compared to the labor of everyone. In Communism, such limitations are removed; production can be done for the sake of self-satisfaction, and is no longer compared to all other forms of labor, only to labor of the same kind. The producers are associated at all times and value is abolished, having all of society's production held in common and all labor recognized with its individual characteristic. Production becomes solely for use, without two-fold, contradictory character.

In a commodity-producing society, commodities can only be exchanged indirectly through intermediaries, as no one will exchange something for an identical copy. This produces a complex social division of labor, shaped by independent producers who must enter the market with differentiated products since producers are forced into the market by social arbitrage, giving rise to a rigid division of labor. Under Communism, this social division of labor dissolves as production is oriented toward communal use rather than exchange, ending the need for intermediated exchange of unique products. While someone might have a particular concrete form of labor they prefer, it is no longer socially required for such relations; even if everyone enters into one form of labor and eschews all others, there is still no social division of labor, but a spontaneous one.

In Capitalism, there is a fundamental contradiction between wealth and value, where producing more material wealth results in each unit holding less value as products no longer require scarcity, a result of higher amounts of social labor time to produce a commodity, to have higher value. This arises from the dual nature of labor: concrete labor (e.g., distinct activities like tailoring or weaving) produces specific use-values and is the source of material wealth along with Nature, which requires concrete labor to convert into useful products, while abstract labor is a social relation that arises when various forms of labor are homogenized through exchange. Through this abstraction process, all concrete forms of labor are reduced to the same “human labor in the abstract,” equating all products to commodities that differ only in quantity. This means that producing more of one commodity can diminish its value relative to others.

Under Capitalism, all products are commodified, meaning they are reduced to exchange values expressed in prices. The homogenization of labor into abstract labor underpins this process, as commodities become valued not for their unique qualities or uses, but rather for their ability to enter the market and participate in exchange. This exchange-based recognition of labor causes labor itself to lose its individual, qualitative character, and all commodities acquire a two-fold nature: as use values (for their physical utility) and as exchange values (for their market-determined worth). Thus, labor is only socially recognized when it results in a commodity that can be exchanged. Products thereby lose their unique, concrete characteristics and become mere units in a world of generalized commodity exchange, alienated from the specificity of their use.

In Communism, this entire abstraction process is abolished. Commodity production, exchange, and money as the “general commodity” are eliminated, meaning there is no need for labor to be recognized through abstract exchange. Instead, labor retains its concrete, individual character and is socially acknowledged for its unique form and usefulness. Without the need to quantify and compare all labor and products through exchange, wealth is produced purely for use, and the antagonism between wealth and value disappears. Social relations are direct, without the mediation of commodities wherein it is only when products are exchanged that individuals come into relation with one another and those relations are determined by the conditions of exchange, and labor is recognized fully in its material form and qualitative value, enabling a fundamentally new mode of production.

In Capitalism, Commodities appear independent of human activity, obeying their own laws and interests- as an example, "the market is doing such-and-such" wherein the “market”, a social force, acts as an entity with agency- and, of their own interest, enter into relation with each other and humanity. This alienates humans, removing their social power over themselves within their life-activity with humans merely responding in a subordinate manner to social processes rather than as agents of history, and leads people to ascribe supernatural qualities to commodities. In Communism, such fetishism ceases, and the products of society hold their real character as social products. Men enter relations with each other directly, instead of having their relations mediated by the world of Commodities.

Under Capitalism, through exchange, values are equated across products which in turn equates the human labor expended upon them. Value, therefore, is a relation between persons expressed as a relation between things. With Exchange abolished under Communism, this relationship between people becomes direct with no mediating intercessor. Thus, Value is abolished, as it only emerges by continual expression of quantities of value in relation to one another that are varied continually independently of the producer, whose social action is alienated into the Commodity which they become subservient to. In Capitalism, Value, or the socially necessary labor time for production of a given class of commodities, becomes what commodities orbit around due to the market and constant motion, mediated by degree of competition, how largely the commodity is produced, and the division of labor leading to complex production chains which result in any super-profits self-cannibalizing from other producers in the long run. Communism, on the other hand, has no need to determine price through this constant fluctuation, competition, etc. Competition is abolished, as is the market. Instead, labor is directly recognized, and society organized accordingly with statistics and direct determination of social need.

In Communism, it is a community of free individuals who labor with the means of production in common. Labor is consciously socialized and combined throughout the whole community. The total product of the community is a social product, made up of products that are useful for the community. One portion of production is to produce new means of production, and remains social. Another portion is consumed as means of subsistence, distributed according to the productive organization of the community and its level of social development; in the lower phase, it is according to the labor time of the individual. In the higher, such limitations to distribution become a fetter to production and consumption, and humanity transcends such (we will go into greater detail about this in the Critique of the Gotha Programme). read later down below, and also Anti-Dühring to some degree. Labor time in this lower phase plays a double part: it is organized according to a social plan which determines needed production to achieve the goals of the community, and it acts as a measure of the portion of the common labor borne by each individual and their share of total product in the means of subsistence. Such a social organization is not utopian in the slightest; rather, it is the most natural form of social organization imaginable. Marx references the actions of Robinson Crusoe, shipwrecked on his fictional island, as an example of this spontaneously occurring, compared to the complexities of Capitalism, which Crusoe could never have carried out despite having come from a Capitalist society.

Lastly, Marx traces the development of religion, which emerges out of material reality in reflection of it and the way we socially organize (rather than us modeling our social organization off of religion, descending from heaven unto earth). For Capitalism, wherein all objects are imbued with supernatural qualities through fetishism and all labor made homogenous, newer forms of Christianity (e.g. Protestantism, Deism, Evangelicalism, Mormonism, etc) are the most fitting; other societies and their social organization produced distinct and fitting religions that reflect the values and interests of that society. In a society wherein the productive power of labor is fully developed, the relations between man and man, man and Nature, are fully developed, comprehensive, and direct, religion and all its trappings of hierarchy can finally disappear, and its negation, atheism, can in turn be negated.

QUOTATION EXTRACTS

To resume, then: In the use value of each commodity there is contained useful labour, i.e., productive activity of a definite kind and exercised with a definite aim. Use values cannot confront each other as commodities, unless the useful labour embodied in them is qualitatively different in each of them. In a community, the produce of which in general takes the form of commodities, i.e., in a community of commodity producers, this qualitative difference between the useful forms of labour that are carried on independently of individual producers, each on their own account, develops into a complex system, a social division of labour.

  1. Social Division of Labor formation

An increase in the quantity of use values is an increase of material wealth. With two coats two men can be clothed, with one coat only one man. Nevertheless, an increased quantity of material wealth may correspond to a simultaneous fall in the magnitude of its value. This antagonistic movement has its origin in the two-fold character of labour. Productive power has reference, of course, only to labour of some useful concrete form, the efficacy of any special productive activity during a given time being dependent on its productiveness. Useful labour becomes, therefore, a more or less abundant source of products, in proportion to the rise or fall of its productiveness. On the other hand, no change in this productiveness affects the labour represented by value. Since productive power is an attribute of the concrete useful forms of labour, of course it can no longer have any bearing on that labour, so soon as we make abstraction from those concrete useful forms. However then productive power may vary, the same labour, exercised during equal periods of time, always yields equal amounts of value. But it will yield, during equal periods of time, different quantities of values in use; more, if the productive power rise, fewer, if it fall. The same change in productive power, which increases the fruitfulness of labour, and, in consequence, the quantity of use values produced by that labour, will diminish the total value of this increased quantity of use values, provided such change shorten the total labour time necessary for their production; and vice versâ.

On the one hand all labour is, speaking physiologically, an expenditure of human labour power, and in its character of identical abstract human labour, it creates and forms the value of commodities. On the other hand, all labour is the expenditure of human labour power in a special form and with a definite aim, and in this, its character of concrete useful labour, it produces use values.

  1. End two-fold character of production because abstraction of labor comes to an end; this is because, with abstraction of labor, different kinds and capacities of labor are made equalized despite differing productivity
  2. Value will reduce as productivity increases

By making the coat the equivalent of the linen, we equate the labour embodied in the former to that in the latter. Now, it is true that the tailoring, which makes the coat, is concrete labour of a different sort from the weaving which makes the linen. But the act of equating it to the weaving, reduces the tailoring to that which is really equal in the two kinds of labour, to their common character of human labour. In this roundabout way, then, the fact is expressed, that weaving also, in so far as it weaves value, has nothing to distinguish it from tailoring, and, consequently, is abstract human labour. It is the expression of equivalence between different sorts of commodities that alone brings into relief the specific character of value-creating labour, and this it does by actually reducing the different varieties of labour embodied in the different kinds of commodities to their common quality of human labour in the abstract

  1. The institution of equivalences through exchange makes it so all forms of concrete labor are abstracted and homogenized to the same character
  2. This reduces all different varieties of labor in all commodities to the common quality of human labor in the abstract: capitalism equalizes all to human labor

The elementary form of value of a commodity is contained in the equation, expressing its value relation to another commodity of a different kind, or in its exchange relation to the same. The value of commodity A, is qualitatively expressed, by the fact that commodity B is directly exchangeable with it. Its value is quantitatively expressed by the fact, that a definite quantity of B is exchangeable with a definite quantity of A. In other words, the value of a commodity obtains independent and definite expression, by taking the form of exchange value. When, at the beginning of this chapter, we said, in common parlance, that a commodity is both a use value and an exchange value, we were, accurately speaking, wrong. A commodity is a use value or object of utility, and a value. It manifests itself as this two-fold thing, that it is, as soon as its value assumes an independent form – viz., the form of exchange value. It never assumes this form when isolated, but only when placed in a value or exchange relation with another commodity of a different kind. When once we know this, such a mode of expression does no harm; it simply serves as an abbreviation.

  1. Value can only be expressed in exchange, when equivalence is determined, with the value of a commodity reflected in another commodity
  2. Hence, the value of a commodity becomes quantitatively expressed as an independent and definite form called an Exchange Value. The form Value takes under Commodity relations is that of Exchange Value
  3. This Commodity manifests as a two-fold character, but only when it achieves this Exchange Value form. When isolated, it is only Use
  4. Thus, under Communism, Exchange is abolished and with it, the Commodity and Value as well: it ceases expression

The third and lastly developed form expresses the values of the whole world of commodities in terms of a single commodity set apart for the purpose, namely, the linen, and thus represents to us their values by means of their equality with linen. The value of every commodity is now, by being equated to linen, not only differentiated from its own use value, but from all other use values generally, and is, by that very fact, expressed as that which is common to all commodities. By this form, commodities are, for the first time, effectively brought into relation with one another as values, or made to appear as exchange values.

  1. In Capitalism, all products of human society are commodified, and then made abstract, homogenized in the form of an exchange value to which all commodities are simply differing in quantities. This exchange value is expressed in the price form, but it turns back and homogenizes labor which is the substance of value.
  2. Hence, in Communism, labor will lose its abstract and homogenized quality, and instead by recognized in full for its individual and qualitative character

The difficulty in forming a concept of the money form, consists in clearly comprehending the universal equivalent form, and as a necessary corollary, the general form of value, form C. The latter is deducible from form B, the expanded form of value, the essential component element of which, we saw, is form A, 20 yards of linen = 1 coat or x commodity A = y commodity B. The simple commodity form is therefore the germ of the money form.

  1. Commodities and Money, both emergent from Exchange, cease

A commodity is therefore a mysterious thing, simply because in it the social character of men’s labour appears to them as an objective character stamped upon the product of that labour; because the relation of the producers to the sum total of their own labour is presented to them as a social relation, existing not between themselves, but between the products of their labour. This is the reason why the products of labour become commodities, social things whose qualities are at the same time perceptible and imperceptible by the senses. In the same way the light from an object is perceived by us not as the subjective excitation of our optic nerve, but as the objective form of something outside the eye itself. But, in the act of seeing, there is at all events, an actual passage of light from one thing to another, from the external object to the eye. There is a physical relation between physical things. But it is different with commodities. There, the existence of the things quâ commodities, and the value relation between the products of labour which stamps them as commodities, have absolutely no connection with their physical properties and with the material relations arising therefrom. There it is a definite social relation between men, that assumes, in their eyes, the fantastic form of a relation between things. In order, therefore, to find an analogy, we must have recourse to the mist-enveloped regions of the religious world. In that world the productions of the human brain appear as independent beings endowed with life, and entering into relation both with one another and the human race. So it is in the world of commodities with the products of men’s hands. This I call the Fetishism which attaches itself to the products of labour, so soon as they are produced as commodities, and which is therefore inseparable from the production of commodities.

This Fetishism of commodities has its origin, as the foregoing analysis has already shown, in the peculiar social character of the labour that produces them.

  1. In Capitalism, the Commodity enters between people in relation to them, and it takes on a perceived supernatural character
  2. Because the value relation between products of labor which turns them into commodities has nothing to do with the physical, useful properties of that commodity, it transforms it into something that is beyond that physical thing
  3. Commodities appear independent of human activity, obeying their own laws and interests (e.g. "the market is doing x") and, of their own interest, enter into relation with eachother and humanity
  4. In Communism, fetishism ceases, and the products of society hold their real character as social products, and men enter relations with eachother directly, instead of having them mediated by Commodities

As a general rule, articles of utility become commodities, only because they are products of the labour of private individuals or groups of individuals who carry on their work independently of each other. The sum total of the labour of all these private individuals forms the aggregate labour of society. Since the producers do not come into social contact with each other until they exchange their products, the specific social character of each producer’s labour does not show itself except in the act of exchange. In other words, the labour of the individual asserts itself as a part of the labour of society, only by means of the relations which the act of exchange establishes directly between the products, and indirectly, through them, between the producers. To the latter, therefore, the relations connecting the labour of one individual with that of the rest appear, not as direct social relations between individuals at work, but as what they really are, material relations between persons and social relations between things. It is only by being exchanged that the products of labour acquire, as values, one uniform social status, distinct from their varied forms of existence as objects of utility. This division of a product into a useful thing and a value becomes practically important, only when exchange has acquired such an extension that useful articles are produced for the purpose of being exchanged, and their character as values has therefore to be taken into account, beforehand, during production. From this moment the labour of the individual producer acquires socially a two-fold character. On the one hand, it must, as a definite useful kind of labour, satisfy a definite social want, and thus hold its place as part and parcel of the collective labour of all, as a branch of a social division of labour that has sprung up spontaneously. On the other hand, it can satisfy the manifold wants of the individual producer himself, only in so far as the mutual exchangeability of all kinds of useful private labour is an established social fact, and therefore the private useful labour of each producer ranks on an equality with that of all others. The equalisation of the most different kinds of labour can be the result only of an abstraction from their inequalities, or of reducing them to their common denominator, viz. expenditure of human labour power or human labour in the abstract. The two-fold social character of the labour of the individual appears to him, when reflected in his brain, only under those forms which are impressed upon that labour in every-day practice by the exchange of products. In this way, the character that his own labour possesses of being socially useful takes the form of the condition, that the product must be not only useful, but useful for others, and the social character that his particular labour has of being the equal of all other particular kinds of labour, takes the form that all the physically different articles that are the products of labour, have one common quality, viz., that of having value.

  1. In Capitalism, producers only come into contact when they exchange their product, and therefore value is not expressed except through the act of exchange. Thus, only in exchange can labor be recognized (e.g. when I make something as a hobbyist, it is not socially recognized. It is only when I put it on the market that it gets seen as labor)
  2. Through exchange, products of labor become homogenous and alienated from their physical characteristics
  3. In Capitalism, as a mode of production which is characterized by general commodity production, production becomes specifically for the purpose of the products being exchanged, resulting in the value being taken into account BEFORE AND DURING production
  4. This causes labor to have a two-fold character. The labor must:

a. Produce a socially useful product (be something which someone desires)

    b. Have an Exchange-Value (equalized to all other kinds of labor, abstract from any inequalities principally via reduction to common denominator as human labor in the abstract, that is as labor-power)

    1. All products, therefore, must be useful for others, and the labor of anyone is compared to the labor of everyone.
    2. In Communism, therefore, the producers are associated at all times and value is abolished, having all of societies production held in common and all labor recognized with its individual characteristics. Production is for use, without two-fold character.

    Hence, when we bring the products of our labour into relation with each other as values, it is not because we see in these articles the material receptacles of homogeneous human labour. Quite the contrary: whenever, by an exchange, we equate as values our different products, by that very act, we also equate, as human labour, the different kinds of labour expended upon them. We are not aware of this, nevertheless we do it.

    When, therefore, Galiani says: Value is a relation between persons – La Ricchezza è una ragione tra due persone, – he ought to have added: a relation between persons expressed as a relation between things. (Galiani: Della Moneta, p. 221, V. III. of Custodi’s collection of Scrittori Classici Italiani di Economia Politica. Parte Moderna, Milano 1803.)

    1. Under Capitalism, through exchange, values are equated across products which in turn equates the human labor expended on them. Value, therefore, is a relation between persons expressed as a relation between things
    2. Under Communism, with Exchange abolished, this relationship between people expressed as a relation between things becomes direct: relations between people without any mediating intercessor. Thus, Value is abolished

    The character of having value, when once impressed upon products, obtains fixity only by reason of their acting and re-acting upon each other as quantities of value. These quantities vary continually, independently of the will, foresight and action of the producers. To them, their own social action takes the form of the action of objects, which rule the producers instead of being ruled by them. It requires a fully developed production of commodities before, from accumulated experience alone, the scientific conviction springs up, that all the different kinds of private labour, which are carried on independently of each other, and yet as spontaneously developed branches of the social division of labour, are continually being reduced to the quantitative proportions in which society requires them. And why? Because, in the midst of all the accidental and ever fluctuating exchange relations between the products, the labour time socially necessary for their production forcibly asserts itself like an over-riding law of Nature

    1. Value only emerges by continual expression of quantities of value in relation to one another
    2. These quantities vary continually independent of the producer, whose social action is alienated into the commodity which they become subservient to
    3. However, through this market and constant motion, these values trend towards their values: the socially necessary labor time for their production
    4. This is mediated through:

    a. Degree of competition

      b. "Experience" of the commodity (how largely it is produced, etc)

        c. The division of labor leading to complex production chains, resulting in any super-profits simply self-cannibalizing from other producers

        1. Communism has no need to determine value through this constant fluctuation, competition, etc. Competition is abolished, as is the market. Instead, labor is directly recognized and society organized accordingly

        The categories of bourgeois economy consist of such like forms. They are forms of thought expressing with social validity the conditions and relations of a definite, historically determined mode of production, viz., the production of commodities. The whole mystery of commodities, all the magic and necromancy that surrounds the products of labour as long as they take the form of commodities, vanishes therefore, so soon as we come to other forms of production.

        1. Commodity Fetishism and the mysteries of it end

        Let us now picture to ourselves, by way of change, a community of free individuals, carrying on their work with the means of production in common, in which the labour power of all the different individuals is consciously applied as the combined labour power of the community. All the characteristics of Robinson’s labour are here repeated, but with this difference, that they are social, instead of individual. Everything produced by him was exclusively the result of his own personal labour, and therefore simply an object of use for himself. The total product of our community is a social product. One portion serves as fresh means of production and remains social. But another portion is consumed by the members as means of subsistence. A distribution of this portion amongst them is consequently necessary. The mode of this distribution will vary with the productive organisation of the community, and the degree of historical development attained by the producers. We will assume, but merely for the sake of a parallel with the production of commodities, that the share of each individual producer in the means of subsistence is determined by his labour time. Labour time would, in that case, play a double part. Its apportionment in accordance with a definite social plan maintains the proper proportion between the different kinds of work to be done and the various wants of the community. On the other hand, it also serves as a measure of the portion of the common labour borne by each individual, and of his share in the part of the total product destined for individual consumption. The social relations of the individual producers, with regard both to their labour and to its products, are in this case perfectly simple and intelligible, and that with regard not only to production but also to distribution.

          In Communism:

          1. It is a community of free individuals who labor with the means of production in common
          2. Labor is consciously socialized and combined throughout the whole community
          3. The total product of the community is a social product of products that are useful for the community
          4. One portion of production is to produce new means of production, and remains social
          5. Another portion is consumed as means of subsistence, distributed according to the productive organization of the community and its social development. (In the lower phase of communism, it is according to the labor time of the individual. In the higher, such limitations to distribution are a fetter to consumption)
          6. Labor time in this lower phase plays a double part: it is organized according to a social plan which determines needed production to achieve the goals of the community, and it acts as a measure of the portion of the common labor borne by each individual and their share of total product in the means of subsistence

          There is nothing utopian about this in the slightest. It is the most natural form of social organization for production imaginable

            The religious world is but the reflex of the real world.[note] And for a society based upon the production of commodities, in which the producers in general enter into social relations with one another by treating their products as commodities and values, whereby they reduce their individual private labour to the standard of homogeneous human labour – for such a society, Christianity with its cultus of abstract man, more especially in its bourgeois developments, Protestantism, Deism, &c., is the most fitting form of religion. In the ancient Asiatic and other ancient modes of production, we find that the conversion of products into commodities, and therefore the conversion of men into producers of commodities, holds a subordinate place, which, however, increases in importance as the primitive communities approach nearer and nearer to their dissolution. Trading nations, properly so called, exist in the ancient world only in its interstices, like the gods of Epicurus in the Intermundia, or like Jews in the pores of Polish society. Those ancient social organisms of production are, as compared with bourgeois society, extremely simple and transparent. But they are founded either on the immature development of man individually, who has not yet severed the umbilical cord that unites him with his fellowmen in a primitive tribal community, or upon direct relations of subjection. They can arise and exist only when the development of the productive power of labour has not risen beyond a low stage, and when, therefore, the social relations within the sphere of material life, between man and man, and between man and Nature, are correspondingly narrow. This narrowness is reflected in the ancient worship of Nature, and in the other elements of the popular religions. The religious reflex of the real world can, in any case, only then finally vanish, when the practical relations of every-day life offer to man none but perfectly intelligible and reasonable relations with regard to his fellowmen and to Nature.

            1. Religion emerges out of material reality in reflection of it and the way we socially organize, not the other way around

            3. Other societies and their social organizations produced distinct and fitting religions that reflect the values and interests of that society
            4. In a society wherein the productive power of labor is fully developed, the relations between man and man, and man and Nature, are fully developed and fully comprehensible and direct, religion and all its trappings of hierarchy and whatnot can finally disappear
