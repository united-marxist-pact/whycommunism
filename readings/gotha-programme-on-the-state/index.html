spoken · the reader's introductionWelcome to the 12th video in the series, The Dictatorship of the Proletariat According to Marx and Engels. Today, we’ll be going over the 1875 piece by Marx, Critique of the Gotha Programme. I’ve subtitled this, “Proletariat’s Brief Reign Defends Revolutionary Gains Through Transformed State.” As noted at the end of the previous one, we already went through the Critique of the Gotha Programme in terms of the character of the communist mode of production. But this one is a significantly shorter section that’s based on, in fact, simply two paragraphs at the end of it. But I have summarized it as —

The State is a product of specific, material social and economic conditions, not an abstract entity that can be defined solely through linguistic manipulation, and it plays a crucial role in the concrete transition from Capitalism to Communism.

During the transition from Capitalism to Communism, the State takes on the specific form of the Revolutionary Dictatorship of the Proletariat, a temporary phase in which the Proletariat consolidates its power and defends its interests against the remnants of the old ruling class.

In this transitional phase, the State carries out social functions that are analogous to those of the present state, but these functions are transformed to serve the interests of the Proletariat and thus the people as a whole, rather than a ruling class.

And this is where we’ll get the first real introduction into — hey, listen, maybe state isn’t necessarily the right word, but it’s still viable to call it that. The example I’ve used historically is the slave picking up the gun to liberate themselves from the slave master. It’s a completely different relation than the slave master holding the gun to enforce slavery. It’s still a gun, right? It’s still something that is being held as a weapon of violence, but its relation and its character is completely different, and this is an unquestionable reality. So it is still correct to call it a state, but Engels is going to call it something else in this, and it’s a very good way to make a distinction, I think, insofar as it is necessary. With that said, thank you for joining me for today’s topical summary, and I hope to see you tomorrow.

QUOTATION EXTRACTS

The question then arises: What transformation will the state undergo in communist society? In other words, what social functions will remain in existence there that are analogous to present state functions? This question can only be answered scientifically, and one does not get a flea-hop nearer to the problem by a thousand-fold combination of the word 'people' with the word 'state'.

Between capitalist and communist society there lies the period of the revolutionary transformation of the one into the other. Corresponding to this is also a political transition period in which the state can be nothing but the revolutionary dictatorship of the proletariat.

  1. The State is a product of specific, material social and economic conditions, not an abstract entity that can be defined solely through linguistic manipulation, and the State plays a role in the concrete transition from Capitalism to Communism
  2. In the transition between Capitalism and Communism, there is a period of revolutionary transformation during which the State takes on the specific form of the Revolutionary Dictatorship of the Proletariat temporarily as the Proletariat consolidates their power and defend their interests against the remnants of the old ruling class
  3. This state carries out social function that remain in existence that are analogous to present state function, but these functions are transformed to serve the interests of the Proletariat and thus the people as a whole rather than a ruling class
